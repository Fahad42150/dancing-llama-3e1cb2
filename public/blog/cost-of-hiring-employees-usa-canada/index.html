Cost of Hiring Employees in the USA and Canada: A Small Business Budget Guide

A salary is only one part of the cost of adding an employee. This guide explains the budget categories U.S. and Canadian small businesses should review before hiring or outsourcing routine work.

The real cost of an employee is more than salary

A hiring budget should include wages, employer payroll contributions, benefits, paid leave, recruitment, onboarding, equipment, software, training, management time and the cost of covering absences. Some items vary by role, location and industry. Calculate the annual cost and the productive capacity you realistically expect—not just the advertised hourly wage.

Hiring costs in the USA: payroll and employer obligations

U.S. employers may need to withhold and deposit federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare taxes, and pay federal unemployment tax. State and local rules, workers' compensation, unemployment insurance, paid leave and benefit requirements can add further obligations. The U.S. Department of Labor explains that federal, state and local employment laws may all apply. Use the IRS and relevant state agencies for current requirements.

Hiring costs in Canada: payroll contributions and provincial rules

Canadian employers generally deduct income tax, Canada Pension Plan or Quebec Pension Plan contributions, and Employment Insurance premiums, and pay the employer portions where required. Quebec has additional provincial considerations, and employment standards differ by province and territory. Consult CRA payroll guidance and the applicable provincial or territorial employment standards authority before preparing a hiring budget.

Compare employee, contractor and outsourced service models

An employee may be the right choice for an ongoing core role requiring close direction, institutional knowledge or in-person duties. A contractor or service provider may suit a defined deliverable or recurring remote process. The correct legal classification depends on the actual working relationship—not the label in a contract. Do not treat outsourcing as a way to avoid employment law.

A simple hiring-versus-outsourcing calculation

Estimate total annual employee cost; estimate productive hours after leave, training and meetings; then calculate cost per productive hour. For outsourced support, include service fees, setup, training, supervision, software and quality checks. Compare like-for-like outputs and include continuity, security and management overhead.

Questions to ask before making a staffing decision

Is the workload predictable? Does it require local presence? Does the role need authority to make decisions? Can the process be documented? What data will the worker access? Who supervises quality? What do local employment and privacy rules require? These answers help determine whether to hire, outsource, automate or combine approaches.
