2026 U.S. Small Business Challenges: Rising Costs, Tariffs and Online Store Operations

Rising input costs and tariff-related uncertainty are putting pressure on U.S. small businesses. Here is how retailers and eCommerce teams can protect execution, improve store operations and keep customer service organized.

Who this guide is for: owners, operators and teams running online stores, apparel businesses and customer-facing companies in the United States and Canada.

What U.S. businesses are dealing with in 2026

The Federal Reserve Banks' 2026 Report on Employer Firms identifies reaching customers and growing sales as the most commonly reported operational challenge, followed by hiring or retaining qualified staff. It also reports that rising costs of goods, services and wages were the most common financial challenge in the prior 12 months; more than four in ten firms reported tariff-related costs as a financial challenge. These findings describe surveyed employer firms, not every U.S. company.

For retailers and product-based businesses, cost pressure can create a second problem: owners and employees have less time to maintain product listings, process orders, answer customer questions and keep online stores accurate. Operational discipline cannot remove tariffs or change market demand, but it can reduce avoidable errors and wasted staff time.

  • More pressure to convert website visitors into paying customers.
  • Higher purchasing and operating costs that make accurate pricing and product data important.
  • Hiring and retention challenges that leave small teams covering more responsibilities.
  • More customer questions about availability, shipping, delivery timing and order status.

How to respond without adding unnecessary overhead

Start by separating strategic decisions from repeatable administrative work. Pricing, sourcing, tariff classification and legal compliance require qualified business, customs or legal advice. But catalog maintenance, order updates, inbox triage and routine store administration can be documented and delegated.

1. Audit product data and store accuracy

Review product titles, descriptions, variants, images, prices and availability. Clear product information reduces avoidable customer confusion and gives shoppers a more consistent buying experience.

2. Standardize order and customer-service workflows

Create clear steps for order checks, shipping updates, common questions, returns routing and escalation. Use approved response templates while sending exceptions, complaints and sensitive cases to the right person.

3. Delegate repeatable eCommerce administration

A trained remote assistant can help with product uploads, collection updates, order administration, customer inbox organization and routine reporting. Define access permissions, approval rules and quality checks before delegating.

4. Track the work that affects customer experience

Monitor listing errors, order-processing delays, first-response time, unresolved tickets and repeat customer questions. A small weekly dashboard can reveal where the process is breaking down.

Choose one workflow that creates frequent interruptions—such as product updates, order status requests or customer inbox triage. Document the current steps, define what can be handled independently, set escalation rules and review a sample of completed work. Expand only after the process is consistent.
