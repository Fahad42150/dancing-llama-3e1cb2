2026 Canadian Small Business Challenges: Hiring, Employee Retention and Workload

Recruiting skilled employees, managing rising costs and retaining capacity are real operating challenges for Canadian businesses. Better workflows and carefully scoped remote support can help teams protect time for higher-value work.

What Canadian business data says

Statistics Canada's third-quarter 2026 business conditions release identified inflation as the most commonly expected obstacle over the next three months (41.6%). Recruiting skilled employees was cited by 25.2%, followed by cost of inputs at 24.1%. The same release reported that 17.2% expected labour-force shortages and 15.5% expected difficulty retaining skilled employees.

A separate 2026 study by the Future Skills Centre, based on interviews with 1,700 Canadian SME owners and executives conducted from March to August 2025, found that 28% had experienced difficulty filling vacancies in the previous six months. The survey also described skill gaps and limited SME resources for training and retention. The dates and survey populations matter: these are indicators of business pressure, not a live count of every current vacancy.

  • Recruiting and onboarding can consume time that small teams do not have.
  • Existing employees may be carrying a mix of specialist work and repetitive administration.
  • Skill gaps can slow catalog updates, customer responses and operational follow-through.
  • Owners need to protect employee capacity while keeping routine work moving.

How to reduce workload pressure while supporting employees

The goal should not be to replace skilled employees with generic automation. It is to make responsibilities clearer, remove avoidable repetition and let employees spend more time on work that requires their judgment, product knowledge and customer relationships.

1. Map employee time before hiring

List recurring activities by frequency, time required, business impact and skill level. Separate specialist decisions from repeatable admin tasks. This helps managers identify whether the bottleneck is headcount, training, unclear ownership or process design.

2. Create documented standard operating procedures

Write short checklists for product entry, artwork updates, order follow-up, customer inbox triage and weekly store checks. Include examples, approval limits and escalation contacts so work can be handed over consistently.

3. Delegate suitable tasks with clear controls

External support can assist with product uploads, data cleanup, order administration and routine support queues. Keep hiring, performance management, sensitive employee matters and final business decisions with your authorized internal team.

4. Improve employee experience through better handoffs

Use one task queue, clear due dates and a named owner for each issue. Reduce repeated status meetings by keeping progress visible. Review workload regularly and ask employees which processes create the most friction.

5. Use AI and automation carefully

Automation can help draft routine text or classify requests, but people should review customer-sensitive, financial, employment-related and exception cases. Avoid placing confidential employee or customer information into tools without approved privacy and security controls.

A simple workload review for managers

Choose a one-week sample and record which tasks interrupt employees most often. Identify the work that needs internal expertise, the work that can be standardized and the work that can be delegated with a checklist. Then test one narrow workflow and review accuracy, turnaround time and employee feedback before scaling it.
