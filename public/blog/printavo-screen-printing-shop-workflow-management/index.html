The operational bottlenecks inside a print shop

Screen printers and apparel decorators handle a chain of linked tasks: quote requests, garment selection, artwork proofing, customer approval, deposits, production scheduling, quality checks, packing and delivery. If job details are scattered across inboxes, whiteboards and spreadsheets, it becomes harder to see what is approved, what is waiting and what is due next.

What Printavo is designed to manage

Printavo describes a print-shop workflow covering quotes, approvals, scheduling, payments and job tracking. Its features page outlines customer approvals, invoices, production scheduling, tasks and job status for screen printing, embroidery, DTG and DTF shops.

Use the software as the shared record for each job. Keep customer contact details, garment style and SKU, quantities by size, decoration locations, ink or thread specifications, artwork revision, approval date, promised due date and delivery instructions attached to the order.

A repeatable job workflow

  1. Quote: capture garment, print locations, colours, quantity breaks, setup charges and deadline assumptions.
  2. Approve: send a clear proof and record the exact approved artwork version and placement.
  3. Schedule: assign a realistic production slot after confirming blank-garment availability and required materials.
  4. Produce: keep job tickets and task ownership visible to the team.
  5. Quality-check: compare finished goods against the approved proof, quantity and size breakdown.
  6. Close: reconcile payment, shipping or pickup details and notify the customer.

Where suppliers fit into production planning

Print-shop management software does not replace supplier stock checks. Before confirming a delivery date, verify the blank garment's style, colour and size availability with your distributor. Common wholesale catalog resources include SanMar, S&S Activewear and alphabroder. Account access and product availability may vary by business and region.
